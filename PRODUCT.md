# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Proposed, not approved: React, Vite, and TypeScript, with Vercel as the proposed host. These are unapproved proposals, not decisions. The repository has no application scaffold, and implementation is not authorized.

## Users

Primary: recruiters, HR, headhunters, and hiring managers deciding whether to interview a frontend engineer. They arrive from a resume, job application, or profile link, scan quickly, and need to judge fit and the quality of the work in a short visit.

## Product Purpose

A personal website for a frontend engineer. It exists to show who the engineer is, the flagship case study, and how to make contact.

Success: a visitor can identify the engineer's professional focus, see the flagship work, and reach email or LinkedIn without a contact form.

## Positioning

Themes the site should be able to support once real work backs them. These are not proven achievements by themselves:

- shipping product features end to end;
- frontend architecture at scale;
- UI craft, interaction, and animation detail;
- design systems and component libraries.

The site is itself a work sample: a visitor judging a frontend engineer is also judging the site they are looking at. Claims stay tied to work the owner supplies, starting with the flagship case study.

## Visual direction

Dark, minimal, editorial, polished, approachable, and career-first.

Homepage mode: Impeccable Experience.

## Language

English and Bahasa Indonesia, with a language switch. The URL strategy for language is undecided.

## Operating Context

Visitors typically open the site from a resume, application, LinkedIn, or email link, often between other candidates, on desktop or phone. They look for professional identity, evidence of work, and a direct contact path.

Do not announce job-search availability.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Homepage hierarchy:

1. Professional identity.
2. Flagship case study: TransJakarta / Lapor Dashboard.
3. Contact.

The CV stays reachable from the hero or navigation only if the file is supplied and approved for public use.

In scope for the initial launch:

- **Identity**: who the engineer is.
- **Flagship case study**: TransJakarta / Lapor Dashboard. Initially use recreated visuals and redacted information until publication permission is confirmed. Do not invent dates, metrics, employment details, screenshots, or permissions.
- **Contact**: email and LinkedIn links. No contact form. The actual URLs still need to be supplied.

Out of the initial launch until real content is selected or available:

- Personal projects.
- Lab experiments, demos, or a playground.

Do not create empty cards, placeholder content, or invented examples for either.

Undecided or requiring confirmation:

- Display name and title wording.
- Email and LinkedIn URLs. GitHub is not a confirmed contact channel.
- Whether a resume file and a headshot exist, and whether either is approved for public use.
- Publication permission for TransJakarta / Lapor Dashboard material.
- Which personal projects, if any, the owner later selects.
- Whether a Lab appears later, and only after a real experiment exists.
- Language URL strategy.
- Approval of the proposed stack and of Vercel.

## Design process

Impeccable is the sole design authority. Other installed design and animation skills are optional references, not parallel authorities. The design critique uses two separate reviewer runs.

## Evidence on Hand

Previously described as available from the owner, and not present in the repository. Public use still requires confirmation:

- Resume / CV with work history.
- A photo or headshot.

Not yet available, and must not be fabricated:

- Publication permission, dates, metrics, employment details, production screenshots, internal names, or logos for the TransJakarta / Lapor Dashboard case study.
- Personal-project entries, links, repositories, or screenshots.
- Lab experiments or demos.
- Contact URLs.
- Testimonials, employer logos, metrics, or endorsements.

## Product Principles

1. **The site is the strongest work sample.** Its build quality, performance, and interaction detail are part of the pitch, not decoration around it.
2. **Respect the visitor's time.** Professional identity, the flagship case study, and contact come first and stay easy to reach.
3. **Show, then tell.** The positioning themes are supported only by real supplied work, starting with the flagship case study. Unsupported claims stay unproven.
4. **Only real content.** Every project, metric, and credential is something the owner supplied and, where needed, approved for publication. Missing work stays out of the launch.
